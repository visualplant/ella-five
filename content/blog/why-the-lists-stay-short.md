I keep five lists, and each one stops at five people. Technical, Design, Research, Product, Growth. That is twenty-five names on the [5×5](list:5x5) this week. I could make them longer. I don’t.

A directory is easy to start and hard to actually finish. Once a list stretches past a screen, I find myself skimming for names I already know and missing the ones I came to find. Five is small enough to hold in your head. You can read a column top to bottom, notice who sits next to whom, and still have the patience to open a card and check my sources.

The number is also a constraint on me. If I want to add someone, someone else rotates off. The lists are supposed to keep up with the work, not harden into a hall of fame. Last week’s board is already in the [[list:archive]]. This week’s grid, compiled on November 23, is new.

I started this because models get the headlines, and the people who ship, design, prove, or explain the work are often a level or two below them. Being on a list means I think someone is excellent at what they do. It does not mean they are looking for a job. Every claim I make traces back to public work, and the sources live on the cards, not in this post.

The short grid has a side effect I like: you start to see the same product from more than one chair.

This week, [[Annie Zhou]] leads design for Project Genie on the [[list:design]] list. On [[list:research]], [[Hang Qi]] is a co-author of Genie 4. On [[list:product]], [[Diego Rivas]] is the product lead who launched Genie 4 in Project Genie on November 18. One world model, three lists, three cards. If each list were thirty names long, I am not sure you would still notice.

The same thing happens with Replit Missions, which launched on November 17. [[Carmah Hawwari]] designed the experience, and she is on Design. [[Samuel Spitz]] is on the product team behind it, and he is on Product. You do not have to take my word for how those jobs fit together. Their profiles are one click away.

I also keep a Visibility level on each card: Under the radar, Emerging, Recognized, Prominent, or Iconic. That is about how widely someone is already known, not about how good they are. Talent, for me, is the list itself. I will not re-rank anyone in a post.

The contrast is useful, though. [[Jason Wei]], now Chief Scientist at Mercor, is on [[list:research]] this week, and his Visibility is Prominent. Most of the Design column is Under the radar, including people leading work you have probably already heard of. Both belong on a short list. That is a lot of the point.

If you only have a few minutes, read one column. If you have a little more, pick a person and follow them off the board. That is why I keep it short.
