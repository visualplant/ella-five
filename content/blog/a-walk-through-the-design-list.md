The current [[list:design]] list is for people who define how the rest of us experience AI products. I compiled it on November 23. This is a walk down that column, in the same order you will meet them on the [5×5](list:5x5). It is not a new set of picks, and it does not change anyone’s place.

What caught my eye, first, was the calendar. In the space of a few days in mid-November, four of these five people had a public launch or a public move. That is a lot of design work landing at once, which is exactly when a short list is useful.

[[Katarina Batina]] sits at the top. She joined Block in November to lead product design at Square, and she announced the move on November 18. Before that she was VP of Design at Shopify, after design roles at Meta, Artsy and ClassPass. In May she talked through Shopify’s bets on the shopping experience on Dive Club. I think of her as someone who has already led commerce design at Shopify and is now doing it from behind the counter at Square.

[[Geoff Barnes]] is Head of Design at Tavus, a role he has held since July 2025. That would be a clean headline on its own. The path to it is stranger, and better. He earned an MFA in painting at the University of Oregon, then spent eleven years at Google, from Assistant to Bard to Gemini, finishing as UX Lead for Gemini at Google DeepMind. Along the way he led the team that designed Deep Research. At Tavus he led the team that launched PAL Maker, a way to build your own conversational video companion. A painter’s eye, applied to talking software.

[[Carmah Hawwari]] designs and builds at Replit as a Member of Technical Staff, and she designed the experience for Replit Missions, which launched on November 17. You set a goal and a budget, and a swarm of agents does the work. She earned a B.S. in Design Engineering at Stanford in 2025, the same year she led the Apple Maps web redesign as a product design intern, then did AI design work at Vectara before joining Replit. If you want the product-side view of Missions, [[Samuel Spitz]] is on the [[list:product]] list this week, on the team behind the same launch.

[[Annie Zhou]] is a Staff UX Designer at Google and the design lead for Project Genie, Google DeepMind’s world-model experience. On November 18, Project Genie added Genie 4, with real-time editing in natural language, native audio, a Project Genie agent, and a community gallery. She got there after designing for Assistant, Google Pay, Google Go, and Fitbit and Pixel Watch. The research and product chairs for the same launch are on this board too: [[Hang Qi]] on [[list:research]], and [[Diego Rivas]] on Product.

[[David Pasztor]] founded UX studio in Budapest in 2013 and grew it into a design partner with more than 300 finished projects, in a dozen countries. On November 17 his team launched Arca, a coding agent built by designers for designers. It works with a product’s real components and turns approved ideas into code. A decade of agency craft, pointed at the last mile between a mock and something that ships.

One more thing, because it is on the cards and it is easy to misread. Four of these five people have a Visibility of Under the radar. Katarina is Recognized. Visibility is how widely someone is already known. It is not a score for talent, and it does not change this order. I put them on the list because of the work.

If you want sources, they are on the profiles. If you want the rest of the week, the full grid is on the 5×5.
