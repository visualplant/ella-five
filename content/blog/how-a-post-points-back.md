This blog is new, so I want to be clear about what it is for. The [5×5](list:5x5) is still the source of truth for who is on a list this week. A post is a place to think out loud, and then point you home.

That means a name in a note should take you to the same card you would open from the grid. [[Samuel Spitz]] here is the same person as Samuel on [[list:product]]. [[Rourke McNamara]], who leads product marketing for Codex and ChatGPT Work at OpenAI, is the same person as Rourke on [[list:growth]]. If I write about someone, I want you one click from their role, their status, and the public sources behind both.

The board does something a single profile cannot: it lets you step sideways. A post should make that easy too.

Replit Missions launched on November 17. I can tell you that [[Carmah Hawwari]] designed the experience, and that Samuel was on the product team behind it. Those are two lists, Design and Product, and two cards. The useful move is to read them together, then go back to [[list:design]] or [[list:product]] if you want the rest of the column.

Project Genie is a wider walk. [[Annie Zhou]] leads the design. [[Hang Qi]] co-wrote the Genie 4 announcement on October 27, and Genie 4 reached the product on November 18. [[Diego Rivas]] is the product lead who launched it there, with real-time editing, a Project Genie agent, and a community gallery. One release, three disciplines. I would rather send you to those three cards than try to retell their sources in a paragraph.

Sometimes the link is a company, and the dates line up on the same day. On November 23, Figma announced that its agent is available in FigJam and Figma Slides. [[Jinen Kamdar]], Director of Product, made that announcement, and he is on the Product list. The same day’s post named [[Johnny Dallas]] as the engineering lead on Figma Specs, and showed him using the agent to turn critique in FigJam into a draft spec. Johnny is on [[list:technical]]. If you only read one column, you would miss the other half of the sentence.

I will also point you to earlier boards when the context is there. The [[list:archive]] keeps previous editions, including the November 16 list that this week replaced. A post is not a changelog, and it is not a new subscribe list. If you want an email when a column changes, that still lives on Subscribe, and it is still one note per change.

A few things I will leave on the cards, on purpose. Statuses describe public roles, not whether anyone is available. Verified badges, when they appear, are explained on the profile. So is Visibility: Under the radar, Emerging, Recognized, Prominent, or Iconic, which is how widely known someone is today. It never changes the order of a list, and I will not use a post to relitigate it.

Related lists, at the bottom of a note, are a gentle suggestion. They are not a second ranking. Start wherever is useful, and if you get lost, come back to the grid.
